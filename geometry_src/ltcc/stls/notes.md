# LTCC `stls/` CAD meshes

These STL meshes are CAD rows in the same SQLite `ltcc` system as the native geometry. Their placement
is authored in [`cad__default.yaml`](cad__default.yaml) (one entry per STL) and uploaded by
[`../ltcc.py`](../ltcc.py); GEMC chooses the CAD builder from each row's `solid = CAD` value. The meshes are
regenerated by
[`../build_cad_meshes.py`](../build_cad_meshes.py):

```bash
PYTHONPATH=../../../../pygemc/src python3 ../build_cad_meshes.py          # rebuild the STL meshes
PYTHONPATH=../../../../pygemc/src python3 ../build_cad_meshes.py --yaml   # rewrite cad__default.yaml
```

## Placement — inside the sector-3 gas mother

All these volumes are daughters of the sector-3 gas volume `ltccS3` (not the world). All six sector gas
mothers are **identical** (`../geometry.py`): the shape the EC sectors use — an **EC-style triangular
wedge-trap** (a `G4Trap` whose inner face tapers to a ~point on the beam axis and whose outer face spans the
sector width, so its side faces lie just inside the ±30° wedge lines). It spans rho 0..4.35 m, z 2.66..5.18 m,
|phi| ≲ 29.9° — wide enough to hold sector 3's CAD frame while keeping a small gap to the neighbouring
sectors. The cones keep their existing `ltccS3`-local placement; the walls and nose carry the local placement
obtained by re-parenting their mesh-build world placement into `ltccS3` (see `CAD_*` in
`build_cad_meshes.py`). Verified with `gemc … -check_overlaps=1`: all six mothers report no overlap and there
are no sector-to-sector overlaps; every mirror, PMT, shield and CAD piece sits inside its mother, and the
reshape cut the pre-existing gas-container pokes from 152 to 29. (The residual 29 are wide native hyp mirror
gas-containers, segments 11-14, that extend a few mm past the 60° sector edge — a preserved GEMC2 quirk no
sector-bounded mother can hold without overlapping its neighbours.)

## Meshes

- **`WC_{S,M,L}`** — the Winston-cone mirrors, cured and decimated (~1500 facets) from
  `clas12Tags/geometry_source/ltcc/cad/`. They keep their genus-1 aperture and carry the `ltcc_AlMgF2`
  optical surface (`mirror:` in the YAML), placed inside the `ltccS3` sector.
- **`backwall`** — the sector-3 frame back assembly (`S1-BW`, `S1-TB`, `S1-BB`), built as one watertight
  solid **plate** with the central gap between the top and bottom bars filled in (see below).
- **`rightwall`** — the sector-3 frame right side, one watertight solid merged from the wall panel `S1-RW`
  and its two rails `S1-BRB` (bottom) and `S1-TRB` (top).
- **`leftwall`** — the sector-3 frame left side: `rightwall` mirrored across the detector centre plane
  (see below). No new source pieces; it is generated from `rightwall`.
- **`nose`** — the nose support structure: five pieces (`Epoxy`, `FrontPlate`, `Mount`, `NFrame`,
  `Nose`) approximated by **one simple filled solid** (see below). Its material `ltcc_nose` is a
  homogenised steel+epoxy mix (see below and `../materials.py`).
  **Note:** the original nose CAD drawings have been *modified* (simplified and trimmed) to fit the nose
  inside the sector box — it is a deliberate approximation, not a faithful copy of the parts. It is clipped
  to the sector and the side-wall regions are cut out of it with a clearance gap, so no part sticks out
  through or touches the left/right walls (verified with Geant4's overlap checker — the nose reports no
  overlap with any volume).

The merged-wall source pieces are read straight from `clas12Tags/geometry_source/ltcc/cad` and
`cadTempRemoved` (see the `WALLS` table in `build_cad_meshes.py`); they are not stored in this repository.

## How the merged walls are built (`build_cad_meshes.py`)

Each source piece is a tilted, drilled thin shape. It is reconstructed cleanly by `flatten_pca`:

1. find the piece's principal axes (PCA); the smallest principal extent is the thin direction (the
   axis-aligned bounding box is misleading because the pieces are tilted);
2. take the 2-D convex outline in the plane perpendicular to that thin axis — this drops the interior
   bolt holes, so the reconstructed face is smooth "as if the holes were never there";
3. extrude the outline through the chosen thickness, back in world coordinates.

The reconstruction is chosen per part (the `WALLS` table):

- **`full`** — extrude through the full (thin) principal extent. Correct for genuinely flat panels
  (`S1-BW`, `S1-RW`) and flat plates whose extent already is the material thickness (`S1-BRB`).
- **`vol`** — extrude through the true average thickness (de-holed volume / footprint area). For *bent*
  thin plates (`S1-TB`, `S1-BB`) whose principal extent spans well past the material and would otherwise
  make a solid block that is far too thick.
- **`cure`** — skip the flattening and keep the piece's real 3-D shape, only closing its open boundary.
  For genuine structural beams (`S1-TRB`, a thin-walled truss) that a flattened slab or a filled envelope
  would misrepresent as a solid bar.

The clean per-part solids overlap where they join, so they are merged (boolean union) into a single
closed surface — no internal walls, no physical gaps. Each result is watertight, genus 0, a single
connected component (~140–440 facets), and Geant4 loads it with no tessellated-solid warnings.

### Filling a hollow frame (`fill: True`)

The `backwall` parts are three narrow bars, so merging them leaves a hollow quadrilateral frame with a big
central gap between the top and bottom bars. Setting `fill: True` on the wall closes that gap into a solid
plate: `_gap_filler` takes the convex outline of *all* the parts together and extrudes it through the
assembly's full thickness. Because that plate already contains every bar, it *is* the finished solid, so it
is emitted directly — no boolean union of the bars with their enclosing plate (which would leave coplanar,
non-manifold artefacts). The result is a clean 144-facet trapezoidal plate (genus 0, watertight) whose
outline still follows the frame's outer edges; only the hole in the middle is now filled.

`rightwall` does not set `fill`: its `S1-RW` part is already a full wall panel, so there is no gap.

### Mirror-image walls (`MIRROR_WALLS`)

`leftwall` is `rightwall` reflected across the detector centre plane. The LTCC sector spans phi = 90..150°,
so the centre line is phi = 120°; that plane contains the beam axis (z), and the walls are placed on z
(`0, 0, 1273.7 mm`), so the reflection leaves the placement translation unchanged. The copy is therefore
given the **same** placement (rotation + position) as its source and only its *mesh* is reflected.

Passive placement maps a local point to the world as `world = rot⁻¹ · local + T`, so to get a world
reflection `M` (across phi = 120°) while keeping the same `rot`, the mesh is reflected in its own frame by
`S = rot · M · rot⁻¹` (`_mirror_in_local_frame`). The reflection reverses the triangle winding, so
`_save_solid` re-orients it outward. The result places at phi = 90..120° — the exact mirror of the
rightwall's 120..150°, with an identical z–r profile.

### Resolving wall-corner overlaps (`resolve_wall_overlaps`)

The side-wall panels meet the backwall at the frame corners, where the simplified solids interpenetrate a
few mm (Geant4 flagged `leftwall`/`rightwall` overlapping `backwall` by ~5–6 mm at large radius). All walls
share the same placement, so this is fixed directly: the `backwall` (dilated by `WALL_CLEARANCE` for a small
gap) is boolean-subtracted from each side wall (`WALL_OVERLAP_CUTS`), trimming ~0.2 L off each corner. Run
after the walls are built and before the nose. Verified with Geant4's overlap checker: no overlap remains
between any of the walls, the nose, or the cones.

### The `nose` (stacked-trapezoid approximation + homogenised material)

The nose support is five pieces that do **not** boolean-union cleanly (the union shattered into >100
components) and whose internal structure does not matter. The detailed outer surface (a CGAL alpha wrap)
was too busy and a single convex hull too plain, so the nose is approximated by a few simple solids that are
boolean-unioned:

- the compact **head** pieces (`Epoxy`, `FrontPlate`, `Mount`, `Nose`) are pooled and sliced into
  overlapping segments along their long (PCA) axis (boundaries `NOSE_SEG_EDGES`, **finer toward the top** so
  the narrowing neck and shoulder get more structure), each segment convex-hulled;
- **`NFrame`** is a big flat frame, so it is reconstructed as a single **flat slab** (`flatten_pca`, full
  extent) instead of being chopped into the hull — the segmented hull made a weird stepped shape there and
  cut into its material; the flat slab keeps that part flat and covers the frame fully.

The whole solid is then **clipped to the sector** (`NOSE_SECTOR`, a world phi wedge a couple of degrees
inside the phi = 90 / 150 side walls) by boolean-intersecting it with a wedge prism, so the convex/flat
approximations cannot stick out through the left/right walls (the raw parts span only phi 93..147, so this
clips approximation bloat, not real material).

Finally, to guarantee it does not interfere with the side walls, the already-built `rightwall`/`leftwall`
(dilated by `NOSE_CLEARANCE` for a small gap) are **boolean-subtracted** from the nose, and only the largest
resulting component is kept (the cut can shave off a small fragment). Verified with Geant4's overlap checker
(`gemc ... -check_overlaps=1`): the nose reports **no overlap** with any volume. The result is one watertight
solid, genus 2 (the two wall slots).

All five pieces are read from clas12Tags `cadTempRemoved/` (the nose bracket is the clas12Tags `Nose` mesh);
nothing is vendored in this repository.

**Volume comparison** (cured part volumes vs the approximated solid):

| part | volume |
|------|--------|
| Epoxy | 15.3 L |
| FrontPlate + Mount + NFrame + Nose (steel) | 40.4 L |
| **sum of parts (material)** | **55.7 L** |
| **`nose.stl` (head hull + flat NFrame, sector-clipped, walls cut out)** | **88.5 L** (parts × 1.59) |

**Material `ltcc_nose`** — the composition is set by the **epoxy : steel volume ratio** of the parts,
**27.4 % epoxy / 72.6 % steel**. With `rho_epoxy = 1.2` and `rho_steel = 8.0 g/cm³` this is, by mass,
**epoxy 5.36 % / steel 94.64 %** (the epoxy split into C/H/O by a typical resin ratio 76/8/16), for a total
real mass of ~342 kg. Because the approximated solid (88.5 L) is ~1.6× the real material volume, the density
is set to **conserve that mass** over it: `342 kg / 88.5 L = 3.86 g/cm³` — the value used. (The packed
volume-ratio density would be `0.274·1.2 + 0.726·8.0 = 6.14 g/cm³`, but applying it to the oversized solid
would overstate the mass ~1.3×, so it is not used.)

### Why `S1-TRB` needs `cure`, not `full`

`S1-TRB` is the right wall's top bar. It is *not* a plate: it is a ~2.5 m long thin-walled structural
**truss**, whose open cross-section spans ~270×170 mm. It also arrives as an *open shell* (6 boundary
edges, zero enclosed volume), so PCA cannot see a "thin direction".

- Reconstructing it with `full` took the convex outline of that 270×170 mm section and extruded it, i.e.
  it **filled the truss envelope into a solid ~170 mm bar** — a chunky "4×4" nothing like the real part
  (envelope volume ≈ 1.16×10⁸ mm³).
- `cure` instead runs the raw shell through `cure_mesh`, which only closes the 6 open boundary edges and
  makes it watertight **without changing its shape**. The thin walls survive: the cured solid is
  1.67×10⁶ mm³ — about 1.4 % of the filled envelope (~70× less aluminium) — and reads as the real beam.

The cured beam still overlaps the wall panel where they meet, so the boolean union keeps everything as one
connected component; no manual move was needed. This dropped the merged `rightwall` volume from
1.59×10⁸ mm³ (block top bar) to 7.6×10⁷ mm³ (now dominated by the panel and the `S1-BRB` bottom rail).

## Temporary comparison volumes

While a merged wall is being validated, `build_cad_meshes.py` also copies its raw source pieces into
`stls/` verbatim (holes and all) and lists them in green in the YAML, so the merged solid can be overlaid
on the originals in the viewer. These raw `S1-*.stl` copies are git-ignored; empty the `INDIVIDUALS`
list (and delete the copies) when the comparison is done.
