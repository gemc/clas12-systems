# LTCC `stls/` CAD meshes

These STL meshes are loaded by GEMC3's gcad CAD factory as the `stls` system. Their placement is
authored in [`cad__default.yaml`](cad__default.yaml) (one entry per STL) and uploaded by
[`../ltcc.py`](../ltcc.py); the meshes themselves are regenerated by
[`../build_cad_meshes.py`](../build_cad_meshes.py):

```bash
PYTHONPATH=../../../../pygemc/src python3 ../build_cad_meshes.py          # rebuild the STL meshes
PYTHONPATH=../../../../pygemc/src python3 ../build_cad_meshes.py --yaml   # rewrite cad__default.yaml
```

## Meshes

- **`WC_{S,M,L}`** — the Winston-cone mirrors, cured and decimated (~1500 facets) from
  `clas12Tags/geometry_source/ltcc/cad/`. They keep their genus-1 aperture and carry the `ltcc_AlMgF2`
  optical surface (`mirror:` in the YAML), placed inside the `ltccS3` sector.
- **`backwall`** — the sector-3 frame back assembly (`S1-BW`, `S1-TB`, `S1-BB`), built as one watertight
  solid **plate** with the central gap between the top and bottom bars filled in (see below).
- **`rightwall`** — the sector-3 frame right side, one watertight solid merged from the wall panel `S1-RW`
  and its two rails `S1-BRB` (bottom) and `S1-TRB` (top).

The merged-wall source pieces are read straight from `clas12Tags/geometry_source/ltcc/cad` and
`cadTempRemoved` (see the `WALLS` table in `build_cad_meshes.py`); they are not stored in this repository.

## How the merged walls are built (`build_cad_meshes.py`)

Each source piece is a tilted, drilled thin shape. It is reconstructed cleanly by `flatten_pca`:

1. find the piece's principal axes (PCA); the smallest principal extent is the thin direction (the
   axis-aligned bounding box is misleading because the pieces are tilted);
2. take the 2-D convex outline in the plane perpendicular to that thin axis — this drops the interior
   bolt holes, so the reconstructed face is smooth "as if the holes were never there";
3. extrude the outline through the chosen thickness, back in world coordinates.

The reconstruction is chosen per part (the `WALLS` table):

- **`full`** — extrude through the full (thin) principal extent. Correct for genuinely flat panels
  (`S1-BW`, `S1-RW`) and flat plates whose extent already is the material thickness (`S1-BRB`).
- **`vol`** — extrude through the true average thickness (de-holed volume / footprint area). For *bent*
  thin plates (`S1-TB`, `S1-BB`) whose principal extent spans well past the material and would otherwise
  make a solid block that is far too thick.
- **`cure`** — skip the flattening and keep the piece's real 3-D shape, only closing its open boundary.
  For genuine structural beams (`S1-TRB`, a thin-walled truss) that a flattened slab or a filled envelope
  would misrepresent as a solid bar.

The clean per-part solids overlap where they join, so they are merged (boolean union) into a single
closed surface — no internal walls, no physical gaps. Each result is watertight, genus 0, a single
connected component (~140–440 facets), and Geant4 loads it with no tessellated-solid warnings.

### Filling a hollow frame (`fill: True`)

The `backwall` parts are three narrow bars, so merging them leaves a hollow quadrilateral frame with a big
central gap between the top and bottom bars. Setting `fill: True` on the wall closes that gap into a solid
plate: `_gap_filler` takes the convex outline of *all* the parts together and extrudes it through the
assembly's full thickness. Because that plate already contains every bar, it *is* the finished solid, so it
is emitted directly — no boolean union of the bars with their enclosing plate (which would leave coplanar,
non-manifold artefacts). The result is a clean 144-facet trapezoidal plate (genus 0, watertight) whose
outline still follows the frame's outer edges; only the hole in the middle is now filled.

`rightwall` does not set `fill`: its `S1-RW` part is already a full wall panel, so there is no gap.

### Why `S1-TRB` needs `cure`, not `full`

`S1-TRB` is the right wall's top bar. It is *not* a plate: it is a ~2.5 m long thin-walled structural
**truss**, whose open cross-section spans ~270×170 mm. It also arrives as an *open shell* (6 boundary
edges, zero enclosed volume), so PCA cannot see a "thin direction".

- Reconstructing it with `full` took the convex outline of that 270×170 mm section and extruded it, i.e.
  it **filled the truss envelope into a solid ~170 mm bar** — a chunky "4×4" nothing like the real part
  (envelope volume ≈ 1.16×10⁸ mm³).
- `cure` instead runs the raw shell through `cure_mesh`, which only closes the 6 open boundary edges and
  makes it watertight **without changing its shape**. The thin walls survive: the cured solid is
  1.67×10⁶ mm³ — about 1.4 % of the filled envelope (~70× less aluminium) — and reads as the real beam.

The cured beam still overlaps the wall panel where they meet, so the boolean union keeps everything as one
connected component; no manual move was needed. This dropped the merged `rightwall` volume from
1.59×10⁸ mm³ (block top bar) to 7.6×10⁷ mm³ (now dominated by the panel and the `S1-BRB` bottom rail).

## Temporary comparison volumes

While a merged wall is being validated, `build_cad_meshes.py` also copies its raw source pieces into
`stls/` verbatim (holes and all) and lists them in green in the YAML, so the merged solid can be overlaid
on the originals in the viewer. These raw `S1-*.stl` copies are git-ignored; empty the `INDIVIDUALS`
list (and delete the copies) when the comparison is done.
